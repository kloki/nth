Schedules a wakeup: after a delay or at a time of day, you get a new turn with the prompt you leave yourself, as a `<wakeup>` notice. Use it when the user asks you to come back to something later ("check the deploy in 10 minutes", "look at CI at 15:00"), or when you are waiting on something slow that nothing will notify you about and there is nothing else to do meanwhile.

Pass exactly one of:
- `delay_seconds`: how long from now, from 1 to 86400 (24 hours).
- `at`: a local time of day, `HH:MM` or `HH:MM:SS`, meaning its next occurrence (later today, or tomorrow when it has passed), or a full date and time such as `2026-10-11T15:00` (local) or `2026-10-11T15:00:00+02:00`. It must be in the future and within 24 hours.

You do not need to know the time to use it: give the delay or the time you were told, and the result says the current time, when you will wake and how long that is from now.

One wakeup is pending at a time: scheduling another replaces it, and the result says what it replaced. `cancel: true` drops the pending one. Write the `prompt` so that you understand it with nothing else: say what to check and what to do with the answer. Keep `reason` to a few words; the user sees it while the wakeup is pending.

The wakeup lives only as long as nth runs: it is gone when the user quits. When it fires while you are working, the notice reaches you between your steps.
