Ask the user one or more multiple-choice questions while you work, and wait for their answers. Use it to:
1. Gather preferences or requirements
2. Clarify ambiguous instructions
3. Settle implementation choices as you go
4. Offer choices about which direction to take

Usage notes:
- Ask 1 to 4 questions in one call, each with 2 to 4 options.
- Give each question a short `header` of at most 12 characters, such as "Auth" or "Library"; it labels the question's tab.
- Set `multiple: true` when more than one option may be picked.
- Give an option a one-line `description` when its label alone does not say what it means or what it costs.
- The user can always type their own answer instead, so do not add an "Other" or catch-all option.
- If you recommend an option, make it the first and end its label with "(Recommended)".
- The answers come back one line per question. The user may decline to answer; then decide yourself and say what you assumed.
